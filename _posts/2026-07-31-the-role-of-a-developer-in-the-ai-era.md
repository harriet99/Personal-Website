Recently, I have been thinking about how much the way we build software has changed. One of the biggest changes I have noticed is that I spend significantly less time writing code directly. With the help of AI tools, my productivity has increased dramatically. Tasks that used to take hours can now be completed in minutes, allowing developers to experiment more, iterate faster, and ship features much quicker.

This shift is changing the way companies operate. Teams can move faster, ideas can become products sooner, and even people without a traditional technical background can now participate in building software. The barrier between an idea and its implementation is becoming smaller than ever.

However, this also raises an important question: **what makes a great developer in the AI era?**

I believe the answer is not simply the ability to write code. When code generation becomes more accessible, the value of a developer moves toward understanding the bigger picture. Developers need to think beyond individual features and understand how each technical decision impacts the entire system, the product, and ultimately the users.

A single design decision can have consequences far beyond the code itself. Choosing a certain architecture, data model, or API design may affect scalability, maintainability, user experience, and the team's ability to move quickly in the future. Great developers are not only people who can implement solutions, but also people who can anticipate the long-term impact of their decisions.

For example, building a feature quickly with a simple solution may seem like the right choice today. However, if that decision creates technical debt that slows down future development, the short-term speed may come at a much higher cost. Understanding these trade-offs and making thoughtful decisions is what separates a developer from someone who simply writes code.

AI has made building software faster, but it has also made thinking more important. Developers now have the opportunity to spend less time on syntax and implementation details, and more time focusing on problems, design, and impact.

The developers who thrive in this new era will not be the ones who write the most code. They will be the ones who can identify meaningful problems, make thoughtful decisions, and use technology to build products that create real value for people.
